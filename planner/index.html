The plan: 16:8 fasting with training in the morning, carbs cycled high on the two heaviest lift days, ~0.85 g protein per lb, five short lifting sessions a week (upper/lower/upper/lower/full body), a walk-jog progression to 5K and beyond, meal prep twice a week, and a weekly check-in that adjusts your starch portions to your real rate of loss. It is not medical advice; if you have a condition or take medication, talk to a clinician first.

How the scale will actually move

  • Days 1–14: water first. Dropping carbs and starting fasting empties glycogen, and each gram of glycogen holds ~3 g of water. Expect 6–9 lb down in two weeks; about 5 lb of that is water you don't get credit for as fat. The plan subtracts it from every target.
  • Weeks 3–12: fat. 2.0–2.4 lb a week. The scale will stall for a few days after high-carb days and after Saturday runs (muscle repair holds water). Judge by the weekly average, not Monday morning.
  • Weeks 13–17: slower, deliberately. As you get lighter, maintenance drops and loss slows to ~1.8 lb a week. The check-in tab tightens macros only if you fall below the target band two weeks running.
  • Muscle. With ~0.85 g protein per lb, five lifting days and this rate, expect roughly 85–90% of non-water loss to be fat. Log skeletal muscle from a smart scale if you have one; a falling 7-day average for two weeks is the signal to add carbs.
On loose skin. No diet can promise none. What's within your control: a loss rate of about 1%/week (this plan), high protein, resistance training so muscle fills the space fat leaves, hydration, sleep and not smoking. Age, genetics and how long you've carried the weight do the rest. Most people under 40 losing 10–20% of bodyweight at this rate see skin tighten over the following 6–12 months rather than hang.

Hormones and recovery while cutting

A hard deficit lowers testosterone (and, in women, can disrupt cycles) temporarily; that is physiology, not a flaw in the plan. The things that offset it are already built in, and a few more are worth adding. In order of how much they matter:

  • Losing the fat is the biggest lever. Body fat converts testosterone to estrogen. Dropping 8–10 points of body fat is worth more than every supplement combined; most men see total T climb meaningfully within 6–12 months of a loss like this.
  • Sleep 7.5+ hours. One week at 5 hours drops T by 10–15%. The 21:30 lights-out is a testosterone rule as much as a recovery rule.
  • Don't starve fat. The meals keep dietary fat at 60–85 g a day (never below ~0.35 g per lb of lean mass); the eggs, beef, steak and olive oil are there for hormones, not just calories. The auto-adjust only ever trims starch, never fat or protein.
  • Heavy compound lifts (leg press, RDL, press, row) and the planned refeed (day 60) and deloads blunt the hormonal dip of dieting. Running stays easy-pace; piles of hard cardio on a deficit push the wrong way.
  • Micronutrients that actually matter: vitamin D3 2,000–4,000 IU daily Nov–Mar (you'll get almost none from the sun this time of year in the Northeast), zinc from the beef and yogurt (a 15 mg zinc supplement only if you drop the beef), magnesium 200–400 mg glycinate at night if sleep is poor. Fish oil 2–3 g is a reasonable extra.
  • Alcohol is the one thing that undoes all of the above. Keep it to ≤2 drinks a week during the plan, ideally zero.
  • Skip the "T-booster" aisle. Tribulus, fenugreek, D-aspartic acid and the like show no reliable effect in men with normal levels. Ashwagandha (600 mg KSM-66) has modest evidence for stress and a small T effect; it's the only herbal worth considering.
  • Get a baseline lab. Morning (07:00–09:00) total testosterone, free T, SHBG, estradiol, vitamin D, fasting glucose, lipids, TSH. Repeat at the end of the plan. Low values at baseline, or a missed cycle during the plan, are a conversation with a doctor, not something to diet through.

The clock

Every day keeps the same skeleton: wake 05:15, train 06:00, eat 11:00–19:00, fast 19:00–11:00. If you entered fixed blocks (class or work) in setup, the Today tab moves any meal that collides with them; the fast stays 16 hours or more. Day 1 is Thursday Nov 5: Lower B, high carbs, and the first fasted morning. Wednesday night Nov 4, eat your last meal by 19:00 so the first fast starts on time.

    Fasting rules

    • Window: 11:00–19:00 (16:8). Everything with calories lands in those eight hours. Black coffee, plain tea, water, salt/electrolytes are fine outside it.
    • Training fasted at 06:00: at 05:30 drink 500 ml water with 1 scoop of your Dr. Berg electrolyte powder (1,000 mg potassium, 120 mg magnesium) plus ½ tsp regular salt, because the powder has almost no sodium. It prevents the light-headedness most people blame on fasting. When the tub runs out, ¼ tsp Morton Lite Salt + ½ tsp salt does the same job for pennies.
    • Muscle-first option: if strength falls two weeks in a row, move Meal 1 (yogurt + whey) to 07:15 right after lifting and close the window at 15:15. Same food, same macros, same 16 hours fasted.
    • Sleep: lights out 21:30. A 05:15 alarm on 6 hours of sleep is the fastest way to lose muscle on a cut.

    Standard meals

    Four meals, the same every day of the plan. Amounts are in cups, tablespoons and ounces; meat is raw weight in ounces. Portions are scaled to your calorie target from the setup. The protein is identical every day; only the starch (oats, rice, potato) and a little fat change with the day type. Built for cost: the protein comes from the cheapest sources per gram (yogurt, chicken, eggs, cottage cheese, a beef-and-thigh mix) with no whey, and nothing is cheaper per nutrient without going to beans. Season freely: salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika, cumin, hot sauce, salsa, mustard, vinegar, cinnamon are all free.

    Meal prep · what gets cooked when

    Two sessions a week keep everything inside the 3–4 day fridge window. Sunday is the big cook; Wednesday night is a 25-minute re-up. Nothing in the plan is assembled from scratch on a weekday; every weekday meal is open a container, microwave, eat.

    Eating for study energy

    Fasting plus lower carbs is good for morning focus and bad for the 15:00–17:00 slump if you get the timing wrong. Rules:

    • Fasted mornings are your sharpest hours. Most people find focus is best 07:45–10:30 before the first meal, once the first week of adaptation is done. That block is before class on Mon/Wed and is your longest free stretch on Tue/Thu/Fri: put the hardest material there. Coffee at 05:30 and a second at 09:00 at the latest; none after noon or the 21:30 bedtime won't hold.
    • Meal 1 at 11:00 is built to not knock you out: protein and yogurt first, oats second, and it's under 750 kcal even on high-carb days. The crash people get after lunch comes from 1,200-kcal starch bombs, not from this.
    • Meal 2 at 14:30 is the study-session fuel. If you have a long afternoon or evening study block, this is the meal to move, not skip: eat it 60–90 minutes before you sit down. On exam-heavy weeks, swap Meal 2's and Meal 3's starch portions so the bigger carb load lands before the study block instead of before bed.
    • Low-carb Sunday is a bad heavy-study day. If Sunday is a big study day for you, swap it with Saturday (Saturday low, Sunday moderate). Calories for the week don't change; the Meals tab day-types are just labels.
    • Brain fog that lasts past week 2 is the signal fasting isn't fitting your schedule. Fix: move the eating window, not the calories. The muscle-first 07:15–15:15 window on the Schedule tab works for people who study in the morning and lift at 06:00; a 12:00–20:00 window works for people who study evenings. Same food, same macros.
    • Hydration and salt cause more "I can't focus" on a cut than carbs do. 3–4 L of water a day, the 05:30 electrolyte drink, and a pinch of salt in a glass of water (or a half scoop of the powder) mid-afternoon if you feel foggy.
    • Creatine monohydrate, 5 g a day, every day (stirred into the Meal 1 yogurt). About $0.10 a day. It helps keep strength and muscle during the deficit and has decent evidence for working memory and mental fatigue, especially when sleep-deprived. The one supplement on this list with strong evidence for both goals; added to the Walmart list.
    • Optional: 200 mg L-theanine with the morning coffee smooths the caffeine; a 10-minute walk between study blocks does more for the next hour than any snack.

    Swaps that keep the macros

    • Chicken breast ↔ 93/7 ground turkey (same weight) ↔ 1 drained can of Kirkland chunk chicken breast (7 oz drained; −7 g protein, −60 kcal vs the raw 8 oz; no prep, keeps 3 years) ↔ canned tuna (2 cans).
    • The beef-thigh mix ↔ all beef or all thigh at the same raw weight (±2 g protein, ±4 g fat). All-thigh is the cheapest; all-beef has the most iron.
    • Potato ↔ rice ↔ oats: 1 large potato ≈ ½ cup dry rice ≈ ⅔ cup oats.
    • Greek yogurt ↔ cottage cheese 1:1. If you want whey back for convenience, 1 scoop replaces ⅔ cup of yogurt at the same protein for about $0.35 more per day.
    • Sirloin ↔ any lean steak (flank, round, top sirloin). NY strip adds ~12 g fat and ribeye/chuck ~15 g per 10 oz: trim the cap and skip the olive oil that night. No steak in the house: 8 oz chicken (or 1 can) + 2 whole eggs + 3 cups veg + the salad with 2 tsp oil matches it within a few grams.

    Nutrients: the veg is 4–5 cups a day (fiber, vitamin C/K, folate), potatoes carry potassium, eggs and beef carry iron, B12, choline, yogurt carries calcium. Add a basic multivitamin and 2–3 g fish oil if budget allows; neither is required for the plan to work.

    Lifting · 06:00 Mon–Fri · 55 minutes

    Upper / Lower / Upper / Lower / Full body, Mon–Fri. Every muscle twice a week with basic lifts, machines welcome. Pick a starting weight you could do for 2 more reps than written. When you hit every rep in every set with clean form, add 5 lb (10 lb on squat and deadlift) the next week. If you miss reps two sessions in a row, drop 10% and build back. Rest 2–3 min on the main lift, 60–90 s on everything else.

    Every exercise has a Watch link (opens a YouTube demo in a new tab). No back squats, deadlifts off the floor, hanging work or hip thrusts: leg press and hack squat carry the squat pattern, RDLs carry the hinge, pulldowns carry the pull-up. Every exercise has three swaps for a crowded gym or a missing machine; the Today tab shows them next to each lift. Pick one swap and stick with it for a phase so the log stays comparable.

    Rules that matter more than the exercise list

    • Daily 100 push-ups + 100 crunches: 4 × 25 spread across the day, never right before an upper-body session. Hands on a bench for the push-ups if 25 straight isn't clean yet; the count matters less than full range. Tick both on the Today tab.
    • Deload weeks 6, 12 and 17: same exercises, 60% of the weight, 2 sets each. These line up with days 36–42, 78–84 and 113–119 so you arrive at each milestone recovered.
    • Strength is the muscle-loss alarm. Log every lift on the Today tab (weight × reps). If the first lift of the day falls for two weeks straight, the check-in adds carbs automatically; if it happens again, take the muscle-first fasting option on the Schedule tab.
    • Fasted lifting: the first week feels flat. By week 2 your body adapts. Salt water at 05:30, caffeine if you use it, and the high-carb day the day before the heaviest sessions are what make it work.
    • Warm-up (8 min): 3 min incline walk, then empty bar × 10, 50% × 5, 70% × 3 on the first lift. Don't skip it.
    • Conditioning: comes from the runs plus Friday's carries and the 10–15 min incline walk after any lift where you have time. No separate circuit day.

    Running · from zero to five miles

    Each running stride loads your joints with about 3× bodyweight, so the first six weeks are mostly walking on purpose. Two runs a week until week 7, then three. Tuesday and Thursday runs are at 18:00 (not the morning you lifted lower body); Saturday is the long one at 08:00 after coffee and salt water, still fasted. Every run: 5 min walk before, 5 min walk after.

    Pace rule: every jog interval is at a pace where you could speak a full sentence. If you can't, you're running too fast, not too weak. Speed comes from dropping weight, not from pushing.

    Groceries

    Computed from your meals: one full week (2 high, 4 moderate, 1 low-carb day) times the people eating. Buy meat and frozen veg monthly in bulk, dairy and produce every two weeks. Cheapest protein per gram, in order: eggs, chicken breast, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken thigh, ground beef, steak.

    Pantry (restock when low)

    • Olive oil, peanut butter, salsa, hot sauce, mustard, vinegar, salt + lite salt (or electrolyte powder), garlic powder, cumin, smoked paprika, cinnamon, black pepper, coffee.
    • Optional: creatine monohydrate 5 g/day, vitamin D3 in winter, fish oil, magnesium glycinate at night.
    • Gear: food scale, 12 three-cup containers, freezer bags, masking tape.

    Meal prep rhythm

    • Sunday 16:00 (~75–90 min): bake the week's chicken, brown the beef-thigh mix with onion and garlic, cook rice, bake potatoes, bag the oats. Covers Mon–Wed.
    • Wednesday 19:15 (~25 min): second batch of chicken, beef-thigh, rice and potatoes for Thu–Sat.
    • Daily, 60 seconds: yogurt bowl and cottage cheese are scooped day-of; frozen veg goes from the bag to the microwave.
    • Cooked meat and rice keep 4 days in the fridge; freeze anything beyond that the day it's cooked.

    Sunday check-in

    Every Sunday at 07:00, before coffee, after the bathroom: weigh in, measure waist at the navel and neck just below the Adam's apple, and rate the week. If you've been filling the morning log on the Today tab, weight and waist prefill with the 7-day average, which is the number the adjustment should use. Save it and the plan recalculates your body-fat estimate, compares your rate of loss against target, and shifts the starch portions on the Meals tab if needed. Protein never changes.

    How the auto-adjust decides

    • Weeks 1–2: no changes. Water weight is leaving; nothing you see yet is fat rate.
    • Target band: 0.7–1.3% of bodyweight per week, judged on the average of the last two weeks (that smooths one bad weigh-in).
    • Below 0.7% with adherence ≥ 85%: one step down (−150 kcal, taken from oats/rice/potato on the high and moderate days). Below 85% adherence: no change, fix the week first.
    • Above 1.5%, or strength down two weeks straight: one step up (+150 kcal of starch). Losing faster than that costs muscle.
    • Floor: three steps down (≈ 1,700 kcal average) is the floor. If you're there and still stalled, the fix is a 7-day maintenance break at ~2,700 kcal, not less food.
    • Protein: stays at ~200 g until you're under 240 lb, then 190 g (drop the Sunday eggs and 25 g of chicken). It is never cut to make calories fit.
